This is **Figure 1** from the referenced source.

The figure illustrates the **distillation range of petroleum fractions**, specifically detailing the composition of gasoline and its various fractions obtained through fractional distillation.

Here's a breakdown of the labeled components:

- **Gasoline**: The top fraction, representing the lightest and most volatile components.
- **Naptha C**: A fraction with a lower boiling point than gasoline but higher than Naptha B.
- **Naptha B**: A fraction with a lower boiling point than Naptha A but higher than Naptha C.
- **Naptha A**: The heaviest fraction among the naphthas.
- **Kerosene**: A significant portion of the distillate, with a wide boiling range.
- **Lubricating Oils**: Fractions that are heavier than kerosene and are used for lubrication purposes.
- **Tar, Paraffin, etc.**: The heaviest fractions, which are typically not useful as fuel and are often discarded or repurposed.

Each fraction is accompanied by two values:
- **Amount**: The percentage contribution to the total distillate.
- **Density**: The temperature range at which the fraction boils, expressed in degrees Fahrenheit.

For example:
- **Naptha C**: 10.9%, boiling range 78° to 68°F.
- **Naptha B**: 2.5%, boiling range 68° to 64°F.
- **Naptha A**: 2%, boiling range 64° to 60°F.
- **Kerosene**: 50%, boiling range 56° to 32°F.
- **Lubricating Oils**: 15%, boiling range 32° to 15°F.
- **Tar, Paraffin, etc.**: 19%, boiling range 15° to 0°F.

The figure visually represents the separation of petroleum into different fractions based on their boiling points, which is crucial for understanding the composition of gasoline and other petroleum products.
