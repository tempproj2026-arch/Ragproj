The Autopneumatic System.—In the autopneumatic system the water is delivered fresh from the well to the faucets. This system consists essentially of an air compressor driven by a small gas engine or electric motor, an air-tight steel air-pressure tank, and one or more autopneumatic pumps. No water tank is required, since nothing is stored but compressed air. The pump consists of two small metallic chambers submerged in the water.

<!-- region:r002_text kind:text -->
When a faucet is opened they automatically fill and discharge, owing to the air pressure from the storage tank. This gives a continuous flow of fresh water.

<!-- region:r003_figure kind:figure -->
This is **Figure 1**, a schematic diagram illustrating a two-cylinder air-water pump system.

### Components:
- **Cylinder A**: The lower cylinder, containing compressed air and water.
- **Cylinder B**: The upper cylinder, also containing compressed air and water.
- **Air Valve Open (C)**: Located on Cylinder A.
- **Air Valve Closed (F)**: Located on Cylinder B.
- **Exhaust Valve Closed (J)**: Located on Cylinder A.
- **Exhaust Valve Open (H)**: Located on Cylinder B.
- **Inlet Valve Open (G)**: Located on Cylinder A.
- **Inlet Valve Closed (E)**: Located on Cylinder B.
- **Compressed Air Tank**: Connected to the top of Cylinder B via pipe K.
- **Water Level Indicators**: Visible within both cylinders.
- **Arrows**: Indicate the direction of air flow and water displacement.

### Inputs:
- Compressed air from the tank connected to Cylinder B through pipe K.

### Outputs:
- Water is expelled from Cylinder A when the exhaust valve is open and the inlet valve is closed.
- Air is exhausted from Cylinder B when the exhaust valve is open and the inlet valve is closed.

### Technical Purpose:
The diagram depicts a basic reciprocating air-water pump mechanism. It shows how the cylinders alternately fill with air and water, compressing the air and expelling water through the exhaust valve. This process repeats, continuously pumping water out of the system. The valves control the flow of air and water between the cylinders and the environment.

Fic. 16.—Principle of operation of an autopneumatic pump.

<!-- region:r005_text kind:text -->
Figure 16 illustrates the principle of operation of the pump. Suppose a small air-tight tank A with inlet valve is submerged in water and allowed to fill. A discharge pipe B is connected at the bottom leading upward to faucet K. Compressed air is forced through pipe C into the top of the tank so that the water is forced out through the discharge pipe until the tank is emptied of water. A similar tank D is connected as shown. The pump has a device for automatically opening and closing the air valves and exhaust valves to the tanks alternately. While A is emptying of water D is filling, and they discharge alternately.

Each pump requires an air-pressure reducer, shut-off and release cocks, pressure gauge, etc. The air-pressure reducer is necessary to reduce the high pressure carried in the tank to the uniform low pressure required to operate the pump. It is placed in the airpipe line between the air tank and the pneumatic pump. It can be adjusted to the proper pressure with an ordinary wrench. The proper working pressure required to operate the pump and raise water to the required height in the building is recorded on the pump gauge placed on the air-pipe line between the reducer and the pump.
